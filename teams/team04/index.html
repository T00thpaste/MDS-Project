BeyondTheBasket

Analysis of Customer Purchasing Behaviour Using Data Mining and Machine Learning. Exploring retail transactions to understand customer value, purchasing patterns, and actionable customer segments.

Problem statement

Understanding customer purchasing behaviour is essential for retailers seeking to improve customer retention, identify valuable customers, and make data-driven business decisions. Transaction data contains useful information about purchasing frequency, spending patterns, product preferences, and customer activity, but these patterns may not be immediately apparent from raw records.

BeyondTheBasket investigates how data mining and machine learning techniques can be applied to retail transaction data to uncover purchasing patterns and segment customers according to their behaviour and monetary value. The goal is to turn transactional data into interpretable insights that can support targeted marketing and customer relationship strategies.

Dataset

The project uses the UCI Online Retail dataset, which contains transactional records from a UK-based online retailer. The original dataset contains 541,909 records, including information such as invoice numbers, product codes, product descriptions, quantities, invoice dates, unit prices, customer identifiers, and countries.

Data preprocessing was performed to prepare the records for analysis. After cleaning, 524,878 records remained. Customer segmentation was conducted on 392,692 cleaned transactions with known CustomerID, representing 4,338 customers.

Approach

The project follows a data analysis pipeline, beginning with data preparation and exploratory analysis before applying customer segmentation techniques.

  • Data preprocessing: Clean the transaction records and prepare the relevant fields for analysis.
  • Exploratory data analysis: Study product quantities, revenue, product correlations, country-level purchasing patterns, and monthly revenue trends.
  • Customer segmentation: Use customer-level behavioural and monetary features with K-Means clustering to identify distinct customer groups.
  • Cluster evaluation: Examine the elbow method and a PCA projection to help evaluate and visualise the four customer segments.
  • Interpretation: Translate the identified purchasing patterns and customer segments into practical retail insights.

Key findings

The analysis identified four customer segments: High-Value Frequent, Inactive Low-Value, Recent Low-Value, and Moderate-Value customers. These groups differ in recency, purchase frequency, and monetary value, providing a basis for differentiated customer engagement strategies.

The exploratory analysis also examines product-level sales, relationships between product purchases, geographic sales patterns, and monthly revenue. November had the highest observed monthly revenue in the analysed period; December represents a partial month in the dataset.

Tools and techniques

Python, pandas, data visualisation, exploratory data analysis, Pearson correlation, K-Means clustering, the elbow method, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA).
