Renewable Energy Adoption and Economic Growth: A Cross-Country Data Analysis

Renewable energy is an important part of the global transition towards sustainable energy systems. However, countries differ considerably in their energy resources, economic conditions, electricity-generation methods and patterns of energy consumption. Our project studies the relationship between renewable electricity adoption, energy consumption and economic development across countries from 1990 to 2024. We use data analysis, visualization, correlation analysis and machine learning to investigate these relationships and identify patterns across countries and over time.

Problem statement

How is renewable energy adoption associated with economic growth, income levels, and energy-use characteristics across countries ?

Countries around the world are transitioning towards renewable energy, but is economic development associated with a greater shift towards cleaner electricity, or does the relationship depend on how countries consume and produce energy? Countries differ in their income levels, energy demand, natural resources, and electricity-generation systems. Understanding these differences is essential to identifying the patterns behind the global energy transition.

Our project investigates how renewable electricity adoption is associated with economic growth, income levels, and energy-use characteristics across countries from 1990 to 2024. Using country-year data, we explore long-term renewable-energy trends, compare countries with different energy profiles, examine relationships between economic and energy indicators, and use machine-learning models to investigate which factors are useful for predicting economic development.

The final outcome is a data-driven analysis that combines interactive visualizations, cross-country comparisons, correlation analysis, and predictive modelling. By comparing Linear Regression with Random Forest Regression, we aim to understand how well selected energy and economic indicators can predict log GDP per capita and which features contribute most to those predictions. Rather than assuming that renewable energy causes economic growth, our project identifies measurable patterns and associations that can help explain the relationship between energy transition and economic development.

Dataset

The project combines two public data sources. Energy variables are obtained from the Our World in Data energy dataset, which provides country-level historical series for primary energy use, electricity generation, renewable technologies, energy intensity, and related indicators. Economic variables are obtained from the World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI) , using country-year observations.

Core Analytical Variables:
Economic variables include GDP, GDP per capita, GDP growth, inflation, and industry value added. Energy variables include primary energy consumption, electricity generation and demand, energy per capita, and energy intensity. Renewable variables include renewable electricity generation and renewable shares. Technology variables include hydro, solar, and wind electricity generation.

Approach

Our project follows a systematic data-science workflow to examine the relationship between renewable energy adoption, energy consumption, and economic development across countries from 1990 to 2024. The methodology combines data preprocessing, exploratory analysis, statistical relationships, feature engineering, and predictive modelling.

Project Workflow

  • Data Collection and Integration: The project uses a country-year dataset containing 7,606 original observations from 220 countries and entities over the period 1990–2024. It includes historical energy indicators from Our World in Data and economic indicators included in the combined dataset. Country name, ISO code, and year are used to identify and organize individual observations.
  • Data Preprocessing and Cleaning: We inspected the dataset using Python and pandas, examined missing values, and checked for duplicate records and country-year combinations. No duplicates were found. Three commodity-price columns for oil, natural gas, and coal were removed because they contained 100% missing values.
    Separate datasets were prepared for analyses requiring complete observations. The complete-case dataset for the selected core variables contained 3,486 observations, while the machine-learning dataset contained 4,013 observations after selecting the required variables and excluding incomplete records. Missing values were not arbitrarily replaced with zeros or averages.
  • Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Exploratory analysis was performed to understand variable distributions and identify patterns across countries and years. Yearly trends in renewable electricity share and GDP growth were plotted. Country-level averages were also used to compare renewable electricity adoption across countries.
  • Correlation and Statistical Analysis: Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients were calculated for selected economic and energy variables. Correlation matrices and scatter plots were used to examine relationships involving renewable electricity share, GDP per capita, energy intensity, and energy consumption per capita. Since absolute GDP and energy consumption are influenced by country size, the relationships were interpreted carefully. Correlation indicates association, not proof of causation.
  • Feature Engineering: Logarithmic transformations using the log1p() function were applied to GDP per capita, energy per capita, energy intensity, and primary energy consumption. These transformations help handle skewed distributions and differences in scale. The transformed variables were used to prepare the inputs and target for predictive modelling
  • Machine Learning: We implemented Linear Regression and Random Forest Regression to predict log GDP per capita using selected energy and economic features. The models use renewable electricity share, transformed energy variables, inflation, and industry value added as input features.
    Model Purpose Method
    Linear Regression Predict log GDP per capita Models a linear relationship between input variables and the target.
    Random Forest Regression Predict log GDP per capita Combines multiple decision trees to model potentially non-linear relationships.

    The input features include renewable electricity share, log energy per capita, log energy intensity, log primary energy consumption, inflation, and industry value added. The target variable is log GDP per capita.
  • Model Evaluation and Interpretation: We used a chronological split, training on observations from 1990–2019 and testing on observations from 2020–2024. Model performance was evaluated using Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and R2. Feature-importance analysis and actual-versus-predicted plots were also examined to understand predictive performance.

Tools used

The computational workflow is implemented in a Python/Jupyter environment using pandas for data manipulation and matplotlib or seaborn for visualisation. Scikit-learn is used for model fitting and evaluation. Each preprocessing step is implemented as explicit notebook code so that the cleaned dataset can be regenerated from the raw inputs.

  • Python, Jupyter Notebook, pandas and NumPy: Used for data cleaning, numerical operations, missing-value analysis, and feature engineering.
  • Matplotlib and Seaborn: Used to create trend graphs, scatter plots, country comparisons, and correlation heatmaps.
  • scikit-learn: Used to implement Linear Regression and Random Forest Regression, and evaluate models using MAE, RMSE, and R2.
