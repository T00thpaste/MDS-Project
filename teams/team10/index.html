FinRisk: Loan Default Prediction

Predicting which loan applicants are likely to default, using statistically grounded EDA, tuned gradient boosting, tabular deep learning and explicit control of target leakage.

Problem statement

Lenders need to identify applicants who are likely to default before extending credit. Our task was to build and evaluate models that predict loan default from applicant demographic, credit-bureau and repayment features, while being careful that the dataset's own scoring formula does not leak into the model.

The final outcome is a set of calibrated, cost-aware default-risk models, compared on discrimination (ROC/PR-AUC), calibration, business-relevant decision thresholds, explainability and fairness across subgroups.

Dataset

The FinRisk dataset contains 230,633 loan applicants described by 61 demographic, credit-bureau and repayment features, with an 8.46% default rate. Re-implementing the dataset's built-in risk score showed it is a formula of 13 of the supplied features (R² = 0.926, residual standard deviation 0.0200, matching the injected noise), so those 13 features are removed before modelling to avoid leakage.

Approach

We moved from statistical exploration to leakage-aware modelling in four stages:

  • Extended EDA: Mann-Whitney U, Kolmogorov-Smirnov and chi-square tests with Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery correction, Information Value, mutual information, variance-inflation factors, PCA and t-SNE.
  • Tuned gradient boosting: grid-searched logistic regression, and LightGBM/XGBoost tuned with Optuna (TPE sampler, median pruning) under 5-fold stratified cross-validation.
  • Tabular deep learning: an MLP with categorical embeddings, a ResNet-MLP and an FT-Transformer, each tuned and cross-validated.
  • Ensembles and evaluation: rank-average, AUC-weighted and stacked ensembles, Platt/isotonic calibration, cost-sensitive decision thresholds, TreeSHAP explanations and a fairness audit.

Tools used

Python, pandas, scikit-learn, LightGBM, XGBoost, PyTorch, Optuna, SHAP and matplotlib.
