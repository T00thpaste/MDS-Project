Hybrid Station Clustering & Cluster-Guided Forecasting of Air Pollution

Grouping EPA air-quality monitoring stations by seasonal behaviour, then using those groups to forecast daily ozone and PM2.5.

Problem statement

Air pollution varies sharply across time and place. Some stations see summer peaks driven by photochemical ozone formation, while others see winter peaks when thermal inversions trap PM2.5. Training one model per station overfits and is costly to maintain, while a single global model averages away local seasonal behaviour.

This project follows a hybrid two-stage design. Stage 1 groups stations into behavioural archetypes with unsupervised learning. Stage 2 uses those archetypes to drive multi-horizon forecasts (1 to 30 days ahead), either with one model per cluster or with one global model that takes the cluster label as a feature.

Dataset

US EPA Air Quality System (AQS) daily summary files for ozone (parameter 44201) and PM2.5 (parameter 88101), covering 2020 to May 2026. After keeping comparable measurement durations, valid dates and non-negative concentrations, and excluding exceptional-event records, the cleaned data has 2,345,874 ozone observations from 1,362 stations and 394,168 PM2.5 observations from 785 stations. The combined station-day panel has 2,589,427 rows across 1,888 stations.

Key columns include station identifiers, date, latitude and longitude, arithmetic mean concentration, observation percent, sample duration and parameter code. Where a station-day has duplicate records, the one with the greatest observation percent is kept.

Approach

The pipeline goes from raw AQS files through cleaning and exploratory analysis to clustering and a leakage-safe forecasting setup.

  • Clean the raw data, audit missingness by field and year, and merge ozone and PM2.5 into a station-day panel.
  • Explore distributions, seasonality (Kruskal–Wallis tests), extreme-day risk using station-relative P90/P95 thresholds, and ozone–PM2.5 co-occurrence.
  • Engineer station features such as ACF at lags 7, 30 and 365, STL seasonal amplitude and peak month, then cluster with K-Means and Ward hierarchical clustering, choosing k with silhouette, Calinski–Harabasz and Davies–Bouldin scores.
  • Build lag features (1 to 30 days), shifted rolling statistics and future targets (1, 7, 14, 30 days) with chronological train/validation/test splits to avoid leakage.
  • Plan model comparison (persistence, regularized regression, tree-based models, SARIMAX/Prophet) with walk-forward validation and peak-episode recall.

Tools used

Python, pandas, NumPy, scikit-learn, statsmodels (STL, ACF/PACF), SciPy, matplotlib and seaborn.
